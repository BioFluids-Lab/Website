abstract: 'The development of a theoretical model of valveless pumping and its numerical
  solution is presented in this work, applied for the case of a closed hydraulic loop,
  consisting of a soft and a rigid tube. A periodic compression and decompression
  of the soft tube causes a unidirectional flow, under certain conditions. The integration
  of the governing flow equations (continuity and momentum), over the tube cross-sectional
  area results in a quasi-one-dimensional unsteady model. A system of nonlinear partial
  differential equations of the hyperbolic type is solved numerically, employing three
  finite difference schemes: Lax-Wendroff, MacCormack, and Dispersion Relation Preserving,
  the last being the most accurate one. When the excitation takes place far from the
  midlength of the soft tube, a phase difference between the pressures at the two
  edges of each tube is developed, being in advance the one that is closer to the
  excitation area. Increasing the tube occlusion or the length of the excited part
  of the loop the mean flow rate increases and maximizes at the natural frequency
  of the loop. The direction of the maximum mean flow rate, for a given tube occlusion,
  is from the excitation area toward the edge of the stiff tube, which is located
  closer to the excitation area. Varying the excitation frequency both above and below
  the resonance frequency, local flow rate extremes appear, manifesting the complex
  character of the valveless pumping phenomenon. © 2006 American Institute of Physics.'
