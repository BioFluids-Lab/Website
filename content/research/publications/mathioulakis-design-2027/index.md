abstract: 'This study assesses the feasibility of geothermal cooling for residential
  buildings in hot-humid and arid regions, using Bahrain as a representative case.
  Unlike studies relying on commercial software, this evaluation utilizes a fundamental
  analytical approach based on ASHRAE guidelines to explicitly demonstrate how individual
  parameters influence system sizing. Vertical borehole ground-coupled heat pump systems
  were modeled using local climatic data and subsurface thermal properties. A comprehensive
  sensitivity analysis was performed to isolate the impact of ground thermal conductivity,
  diffusivity, borehole spacing, system operating duration, and circulating water
  temperature on the required heat exchanger length. The analysis identifies ground
  thermal conductivity and circulating water temperature as the primary drivers for
  minimizing system footprint, yielding borehole length reductions of up to 46%. The
  study also quantifies the competing long-term effects on the ground heat exchanger:
  localized thermal buildup expands the required heat exchanger length by 39% over
  time, whereas natural groundwater flow provides a stabilizing 36% reduction. Techno-economic
  analysis indicates that despite elevated capital expenditure driven by drilling
  cost, geothermal cooling can substantially reduce power consumption and greenhouse
  gas emissions, while mitigating local ambient temperatures by rejecting heat into
  the ground. The findings provide a transparent, physics-based design framework that
  demonstrates the technical viability of geothermal cooling in high-ambient-temperature
  regions, provided that long-term ground thermal saturation is managed. © 2026 The
