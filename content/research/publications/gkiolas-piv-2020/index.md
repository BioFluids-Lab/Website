abstract: The present work examines the aerodynamic behavior of a rectangular wing
  (NACA64-418) in a subsonic wind tunnel with a chord c = 500 mm and a span of 1390
  mm during self-sustained pitching and plunging oscillations. The wing elastic axis
  was located 35% of the chord length far from the leading edge and its centre of
  gravity at 46%. At a free speed U∞ = 14.9 m/s (Re = 5.3x105) and after initially
  displacing the wing from its equilibrium position along the plunging axis, the wing
  started oscillating with a frequency of 3.3 Hz (reduced frequency k=ωc/2U∞= 0.35),
  a mean effective angle of 15.5° and amplitude 12.5°. Based on phase-locked 2D-PIV
  and pressure measurements at the midspan plane, the evolution of the flow field
  was recorded. During upstroke the flow was attached at the leading-edge area and
  the lift took values higher than the corresponding static values beyond the static
  stall angle. At the same time a recirculation zone started to develop at the trailing
  edge which expanded upstream with increasing angle. During downstroke, a shear layer
  was formed in the suction side of the leading edge which was detached from the wing
  surface with a relatively high angle. In the rest region of the suction side, the
  flow was moving upstream with velocities which approached U∞ differing from the
  characteristics of stagnant flow. As a result, the lift coefficient was relatively
  high despite the high drag coefficient values. The angle being reduced caused the
  shear layer at the leading edge to approach the wing surface and the flow to tend
  to reattach. © 2020 American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics Inc, AIAA.
