title: 'Left main coronary artery disease vs. left main equivalent disease: a computer-assisted
  hemodynamic comparison'

- Sokrates Tsangaris

publication: '*Hellenic Journal of Cardiology*'
abstract: 'Introduction: Left main coronary artery disease (LM) i.e. the presence
  of luminal diameter stenosis equal to or greater than 50 per cent in the left main
  coronary artery, and left main equivalent disease (LMEQ) i.e. the presence of luminal
  stenoses equal to or greater than 70 per cent in both the proximal left anterior
  descending coronary artery (LAD) and the proximal left circumflex coronary artery
  (LCX), are two disease entities which, because of their unfavorable prognosis, are
  managed surgically. In the present study, by means of computer software developed
  by our group, we calculated left epicardial blood flow and pressure curves and we
  attempted to compare hemodynamically the aforementioned entities, in order to correlate
  the derived results with clinical data revealing that both conditions are characterized
  by an unfavorable clinical course, with LMEQ somewhat less grave thun LM. Methods:
  For the fluid-dynamic study of left epicardial circulation, a mathematical and a
  geometrical model were needed. The mathematical model was based on the one-dimension
  Navier-Stokes equations. The main variables of the model are blood velocity, intracoronary
  blood pressure, blood viscosity and cross-sectional area of the artery along the
  system. At the inlet of the system (left coronary ostium), the aortic pressure is
  imposed. At the distal beds, peripheral resistance directly and linearly dependent
  on the left ventricular pressure, is imposed. The geometrical model was based on
  physiological and autopsy data regarding length, lumen diameter and elastic properties
  of a right coronary artery (RCA)-dominant left epicardial coronary circulation system.
  The location and severity of obstruction caused by the imposed stenoses were selected
  in such a way that the resulting model would simulate LM and LMEQ conditions. Thus,
  for LM, a 1.2 cm-long stenosis causing 80 per cent narrowing of the left main coronary
  artery was introduced; for LMEQ, two stenoses, each 1.5 cm-long and causing 80 per
  cent obstruction, were introduced at the origin of the LAD und LCX, respectively.
  Results: The interposition of a stenosis causes major pressure and flow drops. Blood
  flow is predominantly altered during diastole. Thus, poststenotically, coronary
  blood flow, rather than being 70 per cent diastolic as is the case in the intact
  coronary vascular bed, becomes systolic. This feature probably represents a compensatory
  mechanism in patients with severe perfusion deficits. Both LM and LMEQ reduce overall
  left myocardial blood supply by approximately 50 per cent. In the latter case, however,
  blood supply to the left coronary circulation is 7.2 per cent greater than in the
  former (55 per cent vs. 47.8 per cent). Under normal conditions, the LAD receives
  55.7 per cent of the total amount of blood entering the left main coronary artery.
  In LM, this percentage drops to 53.1, while in LMEQ it is reduced to 46.6. The blood
  flow waveform changes from its original contour to a waveform that consists of a
  systolic bell-shaped part, corresponding to the time interval between the onset
  of ventricular ejection and the aortic valve closure, and a diastolic part which
  is a flow plateau that lasts from the onset of rapid ventricular filling to the
  end of the atrial contraction. Intracoronary blood pressure along the LAD is, in
  every instance, higher in LMEQ than in LM: This difference is greater during systole.
  Compared with its normal values, blood flow to the LCX is reduced to 50 per cent
  in LM and to 54.6 per cent in LMEQ. Along the consecutive segments of the LCX, the
  blood flow waveform changes drastically after the interposition of the stenosis.
  As is the case in the LAD, systolic and diastolic blood flow in the LCX is higher
  in the LMEQ model than in the LM model. Conclusions: Reduction of blood supply to
  the left ventricle by 50 per cent in both LM and LMEQ justifies the name of the
  latter and provides an explanation for the unfavorable natural history of both disorders.
  In the present study, we demonstrated that the reduced blood supply is unevenly
  distributed between the LAD and the LCX.'
