abstract: Abdominal aortic aneurysms (AAA) are a leading cause of sudden death in
  Western civilization (15th cause in the United States), observed in 7-9% of men
  over 65 years old[1], with a mortality rate of about 90% in case of rupture of the
  aneurysm. Despite extensive studies conducted on the biomechanics of the aorta and
  its aneurysms in recent years, this phenomenon remains an unsolved multifactorial
  problem, and modeling is continuously evolving. A widely adopted approach for many
  years has been the creation of personalized digital twins of AAA through the reconstruction
  of CT scans. This enables simulations to be conducted to assess the stress and biomechanical
  behavior of the aneurysm. In the present study, a semi-automated workflow was designed
  for the segmentation of aneurysmal abdominal aortas and the available subregions
  from CT scans originating from a group of 6 AAA cases with various volume ratios
  of ILT to AAA. A pretrained model[2][3] was implemented for the aorta segmentation.
  After meshing and creating local coordinate systems for the aorta wall, a computational
  structural analysis was performed on some of the AAA cases with the wall modeled
  as a Holzapfel-Gasser-Ogden material and the ILT as a linear elastic material.
