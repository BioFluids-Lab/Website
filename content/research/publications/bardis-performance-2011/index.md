abstract: The aim of this study is the quantification of the efficiency of eight swim
  fins, measuring their thrust and transverse (vertical) force via a six-component
  piezoelectric balance, recording simultaneously their shape along the longitudinal
  axis. The fins were submerged in a water tank with zero free stream velocity, and
  were driven through a plunging and pitching mechanism. In the middle of half stroke
  the measured thrust varied for the examined fins between 11.3 N and 27.8 N and the
  corresponding vertical force between 55.8 N and 84.8 N. Fins with short, wide blades
  and stiff side rails proved to be more efficient than those with long, narrow blades
  of half the aspect ratio. Depending on the stiffness of the side rails and that
  of the fin blades, the performance of the low aspect ratio blades was very variable.
